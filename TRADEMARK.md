## Two different rights, and why conflating them was wrong

Josi CE's **code** is licensed under the GNU Affero General Public License
version 3 (see `LICENSE`). Copyright law governs copying, modifying and
distributing that code, and the AGPL grants those rights broadly.

Josi's **name and visual identity** are trademarks. Trademark law governs
something else entirely: whether the public can tell who a product comes from.

An earlier version of this file required that official Josi branding be kept by
every self-hoster. **That requirement was wrong, and it is withdrawn.** It was
wrong in two directions at once:

- It purported to restrict something the AGPL grants. The licence gives every
  recipient the right to modify the software, and the branding assets in the
  application are part of what they received. A distribution condition that
  forbids modifying them is a further restriction, and AGPL §7 makes further
  restrictions removable by downstream recipients — so the term would not have
  survived contact with anyone who read it, while discouraging people who did
  not.
- It got trademark law backwards. Trademark law does not compel a fork to keep
  our mark. It does close to the opposite: a modified version that keeps our
  name and mark is the thing trademark law objects to, because it tells users
  the modified product came from us.

What we can ask for, and what this policy asks for, is narrow: **do not use the
Josi name or marks in a way that misleads people about where software came
from.** That is the whole of it.

## What you may do — no permission needed

**Run it.** Install and run Josi CE, modified or unmodified, for yourself, your
business, or installations you operate for your clients. Nothing in this policy
restricts running the software, and no branding condition applies to a private
installation.

**Redistribute it unmodified, with its branding.** If you are passing on Josi CE
exactly as we published it, the branding is accurate — it did come from us — and
you are welcome to leave it in place. This is the case a distribution-condition
was reaching for, and it needs no condition: it is simply true.

**Modify it.** The AGPL grants this and this policy does not take it back. If you
modify Josi CE, see the section below on what to call the result.

**Refer to Josi by name.** Documentation, tutorials, comparisons, blog posts,
bug reports, conference talks, and statements that your work is *"based on Josi
CE"*, *"a fork of Josi CE"*, or *"compatible with Josi"* — all fine, provided it
is clear you are not the origin of Josi and are not endorsed by us. This is
nominative use and we do not claim the right to stop it.

**Remove our branding from your fork.** Not only permitted — usually the correct
thing to do, and the next section explains why.

## What we ask of a modified version

If you distribute a modified Josi CE, the concern is confusion: a user should be
able to tell whether they are running our software or yours. So:

- **Do not present a modified version as the official Josi product**, or as
  endorsed, supported, certified or approved by SOCAL RECEPTIONIST LLC.
- **Do not use "Josi" as, or as part of, the name** of your fork, product,
  company, domain or app-store listing. "Josi Pro", "Josi Plus", "getjosi.com"
  and similar all say the product is ours.
- **Replace or remove the Josi name and marks in the running application** where
  they identify the product's origin, if your changes are more than cosmetic.
  Describing your product as based on Josi CE is the accurate alternative.

Everything above is a restriction on **trademark use**, not on copying,
modifying or distributing the code. None of it is a condition of the AGPL
licence grant, none of it is a further restriction under AGPL §7, and removing
our branding is expressly permitted rather than forbidden.
